Psychology & Technology

Technology with a human perspective.

An academic minor alongside Computer Science Engineering, in four short chapters.

01

Understanding People

Developmental · Social psychology

Psychology has given me a vocabulary for how people grow, behave and relate to each other. Developmental Psychology shows how context and life stage shape needs; Social Psychology shows how people influence, and are influenced by, the groups around them.

Studying motivation and different perspectives has taught me to ask a better first question: not “what can this system do?” but “who is this for, and what do they actually need?”

02

Understanding Systems

Psychology × Computer Science

Software and AI systems are used by people in real contexts — stressed, rushed, uncertain, or short of information. Understanding users, context and human needs changes what I build and how I explain it.

That is why my projects favour explainability, source tracking and honest limits: a farmer, a researcher or an HR manager should be able to see why a system says what it says.

03

Applying What I Learn

Industrial & Organizational Psychology

The clearest example is my Burnout & Attrition Intelligence System. It uses the Job Demands–Resources (JD-R) model from I/O Psychology as an interpretive frame, explainable machine learning (XGBoost with SHAP) to show what drives a risk estimate, and a RAG pipeline to provide evidence-grounded HR policy guidance.

The full technical story lives on its own case-study page.

04

Technology With a Human Perspective

A reflection

Computational thinking helps me break a problem into parts; psychological learning reminds me that the parts are people. Emerging AI makes it easy to build quickly, and that is exactly when a second perspective matters most.

This is an academic learning journey — I’m a student of both fields, not a psychologist or mental-health professional — and I want my engineering to stay curious about the human side of every problem.
